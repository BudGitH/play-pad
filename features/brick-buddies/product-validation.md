## The gate (how this PASSES)

**Three-tier bar — two tiers gate, one is scored:**

- **Baseline met** — does what the business requirements say **and** each target persona completes their primary goal. **Hard gate.**
- **Value creation** — personas judge it worth using over the status quo. **Hard gate.**
- **High satisfaction** — scored, pursued via `docs/IDEAS.md` over time. **Never a release gate.**

**Blocks (→ round table):** PM verdict `NEEDS_REVISION`, OR any persona CRITICAL tied to a baseline/value miss.
**Logs, doesn't block:** satisfaction feedback + any "critical" not tied to baseline/value → `docs/IDEAS.md` (recurring → `docs/findings-ledger.md` under PRODUCT/UX).

**PASS when ALL hold:** (1) PM = `PASS`; (2) every target persona reports **baseline = met AND value = met**; (3) zero open baseline/value-blocking criticals. Satisfaction is recorded, never required maximal.

**Loop bound (no infinite loop):** the round table iterates **≤ 3 rounds** per feature. If a blocking item survives 3 rounds → stop, log it in `findings-ledger.md`, mark **BLOCKED — ESCALATED**, and surface it to the human.

## How this run was done (read before trusting the scores)

- **Mode: live.** One driver served the repo (`node tools/serve.mjs 5651`) and drove headless Chromium (Playwright, `chrome-linux64` 1243) **starting from the hub at `/`**, tapping/clicking the Brick Buddies card. Real input only: keyboard events and mouse for Dan and Sam (1280x800), CDP touch events (`Input.dispatchTouchEvent`) and `touchscreen.tap` for Maya (390x844 portrait) and Priya & kid (844x390 landscape, plus a 25 s random-tap "child mash" run). Screenshots were taken at every step and looked at.
- **No debug shortcuts for gameplay.** Placing, breaking, walking, climbing, unlocking and building all went through the real input path. `__buddies.debug.tileToScreen` / `getTile` / `checklist` were used **read-only** to aim at tiles and read state (a human aims by eye). Four narrow exceptions, none of which touched a score: `debug.setTime(0.5)` to get a daylight fidelity capture, `debug.save()` before one reload in Dan's persistence check, `debug.teleport()` once to stand in the starter Library (P-2), and `debug.findPath` / `groundRow` to inspect world connectivity and terrain (P-1, P-9).
- **Limits, stated plainly.** (a) A script is not a person: **times below are input counts plus my estimate of human time**, not measured human time. (b) After Dan's exploratory run the later personas knew the route (left toward the "◀ Pip" pointer to the porch), so only Dan's first run is "naive"; every persona still completed request 1 by actually building. (c) **Sound cannot be heard headlessly: the sound design is judged from the code only.** (d) No real children, no real phones, no real train. A clean panel means "no obvious miss", not "validated by users" (`PRODUCT-PANEL.md` caveat).
- **Evidence** (scratchpad, not committed): `…/scratchpad/pv-bb/` — `dan-*`, `maya-*`, `kid-*`, `sam-*`, `ux-*` PNGs. Key frames: `dan-01-start`, `dan-05-backwalls`, `dan-08b-bed-placed`, `dan-25-kitchen-moved`, `maya-04-start`, `maya-21-kitchen-done`, `kid-05-porch` (card overlap), `kid-06-bed-placed`, `sam-07-moved`, `dan-32-starter-library-checklist`, `ux-01-desktop-zoom24`, `ux-03-side-by-side` (reference vs ours), `ux-04-kitchen-row-compare`.

### The owner's three asks

1. **Fidelity to the reference — strong, not exact. ~4/5, owner to judge (AC-16).** Composition, materials and mood match at first glance; three measurable deltas remain (UX section: saturation, cool/dark underground, sparse upper floors / no set-pieces / no rim light). The owner said "I do not accept less": that is the one place this could still turn into a blocker, so it is called out explicitly rather than buried.
2. **Fun, playable mechanics — yes.** The loop works and feels good: free placing with a live ghost (green valid / red box with ✕ and a reason), drag-to-paint rows on mouse **and** touch, a request checklist that re-evaluates the region you stand in ("Pip asks for · 4/6", "Done: / Missing:"), instant "Mabel is heading to the room…" then confetti, a bell chime, +coins and a new buddy within ~5 s. Building a whole room from scratch took ≈ 22 mouse actions (Dan) and ≈ 20 taps (Maya). Weakness: request 1 is almost free (P-1), and depth after request 5 is "grand X" repeats (IDEAS-6).
3. **Sound design — comprehensive on paper (code-reviewed only).** Synthesised, no files: per-material place/break (wood thump, stone tap, glass chime, dirt thud, cloth), surface-dependent footsteps, jump/land, ladder creak, door open/close, soft UI click/error, coin + unlock, a four-note bell move-in chime and a fanfare, portal whoosh, thunder, a rain bed that is muffled indoors, furnace crackle, and a generative pentatonic pad + pluck music loop on its own bus. Tone is cosy and soft (no harsh stingers), toggles exist for Sound/Music/Rain (title, pause menu, `M`, `N`), audio starts only after a gesture and suspends when hidden. **Unverified by ear:** relative levels of rain vs music vs sfx, and whether thunder startles a small child.

(3) Add a 1-tile warm rim light under floors near light sources (additive, tile-resolution is fine). (4) Keep clouds/hills as they are (parity).

Brick Buddies meets its brief: all three success metrics were verified live, it runs from `file://`, saves and shows a "Housed N" badge on the hub, and a first request takes roughly one to three minutes on a laptop, a phone (portrait and landscape) and with the keyboard alone; room-building feels good (live checklist, ghost preview, drag-painting, a generous "moved in" celebration).
