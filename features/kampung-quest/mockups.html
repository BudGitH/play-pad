Kampung Quest — 4 HUD & interaction options

How to choose: pick the shape that fits how the target players actually play: a 7-year-old on a sofa tablet (Priya & kid), one-handed phone sessions on a train (Maya), and keyboard only with captions (Sam). Device and age beat looks. Mixing is allowed ("C's compass with D's picture prayer steps"). All four use the same PlayPad tokens; you are choosing where information lives and how you reach it, not a theme.

Each option shows the same six moments on a phone in landscape (844 × 390) and a desktop (1280 × 720): ① exploring with the "Talk to Nenek" prompt ② talking to Nenek ③ the azan at Zohor ④ the guided prayer steps ⑤ the hawker stall menu ⑥ the option's own "big menu" (journal / phone / map / picture board). Your choice and rationale go in design-review.md §5.

Strategy: the open-world convention: status top-left, mission top-right, minimap bottom-left (desktop), every panel always on. Big tasks (prayer steps, stall menu, journal) open as centred modal dialogs over a dimmed world.

Best when: players already know open-world games; everything is visible at a glance with no taps. Trade-off: the most chrome on screen; on a phone the four corners collide with the thumbs, so the minimap moves up next to the status and the tracker shrinks to one line. Modals hide the world, so the hero's posture during prayer is not seen. Responsive: desktop = 4 corners; phone = status + minimap top-left, joystick bottom-left, actions bottom-right; portrait would stack the top row into one scrolling strip (not recommended).

Strategy: a toy smartphone the hero carries holds missions, map, prayer, the stall's order screen and stickers. The world HUD is only a clock chip, two tiny needs rings, one "next step" line and an edge arrow. The phone slides up on its own when the azan starts.

Best when: the world should look clean and cinematic, and the "kid with a phone" fiction is part of the fun. Trade-off: one more tap to reach anything; no minimap on screen, so wayfinding leans on the arrow; everything is in a narrow portrait phone, so the menu of 10 dishes and 8 missions scroll. Teaching a 7-year-old that prayer lives in a phone app is also a values question. Responsive: desktop = portrait toy phone bottom-right; on a real phone the toy phone turns landscape and fills the screen.

Strategy: one compass strip on top carries direction and every marker (mission, mosque, hawker, home) with distances; one context card at the bottom shows the ONE thing that matters now and changes with context: mission → "Talk to Nenek" → dialogue → azan → prayer steps → stall menu (as a bottom sheet). The world stays visible, so the hero is seen taking each prayer posture.

Best when: you want calm, focused play and the 3D world to carry the premium feel. Trade-off: a compass is more abstract than a map for a young child; only one card at a time means the full mission list is one tap away (the big map). Responsive: desktop = card bottom-centre; phone = the card sits between the joystick and the run button and its action button IS the interact button; portrait = card above the joystick.

Strategy: pictures first, words second. A dock of big labelled icons (Missions, Map, Pray, Bag, Pause), a minimap wrapped in a two-arc needs ring (food and energy), a "day of prayers" strip with all five prayers, and a picture mission card. Talking, praying and eating use big picture tiles with a "read to me" button.

Best when: the main player is 5-9 and still learning to read; Priya can hand it over without explaining. Trade-off: the biggest targets cost space; on a phone the dock and needs ring take the whole top band, and teens/adults may find it young. Picture tiles need more art (dish, posture and mission pictures). Responsive: desktop = dock bottom-centre; phone = dock moves to the top band between the clock and the ring, so the thumbs keep the bottom corners.

Decided: Option D, "Kid dock and needs ring" (owner, 2026-11-26). These frames are the build spec for Phase 2 tests and Phase 3. Rules that apply to every frame:

  • Focus order (keyboard): the nearest action (Talk / Show me the way) → clock and prayers → mission card and its read-to-me → dock (Missions, Map, Pray, Bag, Pause) → mute → needs ring (read-only, announced). Shortcuts E act, 1-4 pick a tile, M map, J missions, P pray, Esc pause. Every dialog traps focus and returns it to the control that opened it.
  • Reduced motion: with prefers-reduced-motion or "Less motion" on, there is no camera bob or shake, no pulse (a steady outline instead), footprints appear at once, cards fade instead of sliding, and meters jump.
  • Captions: the azan always shows Arabic, transliteration and English. Muted sound or a hidden tab means captions only, and nothing plays while paused or hidden.
  • Wudu order (AC-8, Shafi'i): intention, hands ×3, mouth ×3, nose ×3, face ×3, right arm to the elbow ×3, left arm ×3, wipe the head, ears, right foot to the ankle ×3, left foot ×3. A wrong tile gives a hint, never a fail.
  • Edge order: a prayer that comes due during dialogue or a menu shows its card after that closes. Reloading mid-flow resumes at the start of the current step, and completed wudu is kept. Maghrib → Isyak are tracked separately, and the earlier becomes qada.
  • Portrait phone: playable fallback. The dock moves to the bottom row, the joystick and Run sit above it, and a dismissible chip suggests turning sideways. Everything stays inside --pp-app-h.
